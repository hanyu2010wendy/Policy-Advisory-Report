### Building a "Jiangxi Model" of Green Development Education and Cosolidating the Foundation for Green Developemnt: Experience and Insights from Finland

**Authors:** Zhu Limeng, Han Yu

*(Jiangxi University of Finance and Economics, Nanchang 330013, China)*

### Abstract
Jiangxi began relatively early in educating people in the idea of green development, and this has helped the province grow into a national pilot zone for ecological civilization, making it an ideal place to realize General Secretary Xi Jinping’s vision of building a “Jiangxi model” for a Beautiful China. Yet Jiangxi has not established an education system oriented specifically toward green development. This report therefore recommends that the province learn from Finland’s proven experience and, in light of the requirements of green development, formulate a plan for green development education in Jiangxi on the basis of the existing framework of education for sustainable development (ESD), establish a system of green development education, advance green development education from early childhood and initiate young children’s understanding of green development, popularize green development education at the primary and secondary levels so that the concept takes root among adolescents, vigorously promote vocational education for green development so as to cultivate application-oriented talent suited to green development, and fully advance higher education for green development in order to meet the new needs of Jiangxi’s green development and thereby form a distinct “Jiangxi model” of green development education.

### Introduction
The term “green development,” as one of the five development concepts—'innovation, coordination, green development, openness, and sharing'—advanced in the context of socialism with Chinese characteristics entering a new era, is increasingly gaining recognition and acceptance worldwide. Since 2012, the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) has convened a regular annual forum on green growth and sustainable development. This indicates, to some extent, that the concept of “green development,” is exerting a profound global influence. But how can the idea of green development be translated into concrete practice? Education must come first. In China, however, relevant work has been carried out mainly under the banner of education for sustainable development. From the China Agenda 21 issued in 1994 to the National Outline for Medium- and Long-Term Education Reform and Development (2010-2020) released in 2010, ESD has consistently been identified as one of the country’s strategic themes. As early as 1996, China first proposed the concept of the “green school,” and in 2013 it issued the Standards for the Evaluation of Green Campuses. In 2017, the report to the 19th National Congress of the Communist Party of China explicitly called for the advocacy of simple, moderate, green, and low-carbon lifestyles; opposition to extravagance, waste, and irrational consumption; and the launch of initiatives to create conservation-oriented government institutions, green families, green schools, green communities, and green travel. In 2018, the National Development and Reform Commission issued the Work Plan for Initiatives to Create Conservation-Oriented Government Institutions, Green Families, Green Schools, Green Communities, and Green Travel, among other documents. In response, localities across the country also began exploring ways to bring the concept of green development into the classroom. Xining (China), for example, published and distributed a series of school-based teaching materials titled Xining Education: Green Development, with one volume each for lower primary, upper primary, junior secondary, and senior secondary students, centered on cultivating students’ spirit of green development. All of this has laid a strong foundation for green development education. Carrying out green development education is not only a concrete practice for implementing the concept of green development, but also an important lever for contributing Chinese wisdom and experience.

Compared with the rest of the country, Jiangxi began earlier in educating people in the concept of green development. Building a “Jiangxi model” of green development education is not only feasible but can also lay an even firmer foundation for creating a “Jiangxi model” for a Beautiful China and further enrich the meaning of that model. First, making the province strong through ecology and developing through green growth has long been one of Jiangxi’s most consistent development strategies and greatest advantages. It is precisely under the guidance of the idea that “lucid waters and lush mountains are invaluable assets” and the guideline of “innovation-led development, reform breakthroughs, greater openness, green development, practical responsibility, and prosperous development for the people” that Jiangxi has grown into a national pilot zone for ecological civilization and become an ideal place for building a “Jiangxi model” for a Beautiful China. To maintain this advantage, Jiangxi needs the support of green development education. Second, it is both the country’s earnest expectation for Jiangxi and the natural requirement that Jiangxi serve as a model and strive to be a pioneer. Only three years after his previous inspection, General Secretary Xi Jinping again visited Jiangxi for inspection and guidance, further making clear that Jiangxi should accelerate the building of an ecological-civilization system and pursue high-quality development to create a “Jiangxi model” for a Beautiful China and paint a new picture of reform and development in Jiangxi in the new era. It should therefore be Jiangxi’s responsibility to advance education reform, integrate green development education into the education system, and take the lead nationwide in this area. Third, the experience Jiangxi has already accumulated in green development education has laid a foundation for building a “Jiangxi model” of green development education. Although the province has not yet established a dedicated education system oriented toward green development, it has accumulated years of experience in implementing ESD, and seven batches of schools have successively received the title of “Green School” jointly awarded by the Department of Education of Jiangxi Province and the provincial department of ecology and environment.

Jiangxi possesses the preconditions for pioneering green development education. The key question is how to build a “Jiangxi model” of green development education. As noted above, China has already begun practical exploration in this area, but the work is still at an early stage and needs to learn from the experience of developed Western countries. Yet because the term “green development” originated in China, the closest concept used in Western countries is sustainable development, and those countries have achieved great success in education for sustainable development. Finland has been at the global forefront in ESD and can provide Jiangxi with valuable experience and lessons. Finland covers 338,000 square kilometers, with forest coverage as high as 68 percent, earning it the reputation of a “green treasury”; inland waters account for 10 percent of its territory, giving it the title “land of a thousand lakes.” Benefiting from its sound ecological environment and the success of its ESD efforts, Finland has been rated one of the world’s most competitive countries and has reached global leading levels in such fields as energy, telecommunications, biotechnology, and environmental protection. OECD data and related studies show that Finland ranks first among OECD countries in educational efficiency. Finnish 15-year-old students have also performed outstandingly over successive cycles of the Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) in mathematics, reading, and scientific literacy, with scientific literacy in particular ranking among the world’s top five. Like Finland, Jiangxi’s greatest advantages and potential also lie in its ecological environment. If Jiangxi learns from Finland and devotes sustained effort to building a “Jiangxi model” of green development education, it can not only consolidate the foundation for green development but may also become a model for the whole country to learn from and help the Chinese concept of “green development,” as a form of Chinese wisdom and experience, play an even stronger leading role in the world.
